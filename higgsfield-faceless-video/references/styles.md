# Visual styles

Use a user-selected catalog style or explicit reference when supplied. Lock one consistent style and palette across the production. The following are creative starting points, not a fixed catalog; use live preset discovery for actual preset IDs.

- **Editorial collage:** warm cream paper with subtle fiber grain, monochrome halftone cutouts, rough paper edges, one accent color, abstract unlabeled diagrams and maps, soft paper shadows and staggered hand-crafted motion. Keep words in narration unless on-screen text was requested. Useful for explainers and documentary subjects.
- **Paper diorama:** miniature layered cardboard and aged-paper sets, torn edges, restrained halftone texture, a single accent color, warm directional light, shallow depth of field and handcrafted stop-motion movement. Keep recurring props visually stable.
- **Cinematic storybook:** hand-painted 2D illustration, expressive characters, detailed environments, a muted jewel-tone palette and warm light against deep atmospheric shadow. Keep character identity and costume consistent; vary camera scale and action.
- **Mannequin:** clean matte clay figures, simple period-appropriate environments, even neutral light and smooth weighty movement. Use consistent silhouettes and colors for recurring figures.
- **Children's looks:** choose an age-appropriate 3D, flat 2D, hand-drawn or vector look. Give characters recognizable features and distinct reactions; let the visuals show the action named by the narration. Keep the dialogue simple and use an upbeat instrumental bed only when the user wants music.

When a real photo is supplied only as a style donor, do not copy its person or composition. When a user asks to preserve a mascot, create a consistent illustrated reference and check recognizable features before rendering the whole set. Before generating a long sequence, inspect the first completed scene for style, framing, movement and character consistency.
